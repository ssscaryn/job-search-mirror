# Salary Script

Many people never negotiate, and women in particular are trained out of asking and penalised as "difficult" when they do. As a script, it takes about 90 seconds.

## The four moves

1. **Never name the first number.** There is no reason to anchor against yourself. Ask for the band: *"Do you have the band for this role?"*
2. **Make them surface the range.** Keep the recruiter talking about their number, never yours. Some people use a riskier variant: implying the band was missing from the posting. It can shift the power in the conversation, but it can also cost trust. Treat it as high risk and only use it when the room clearly allows it.
3. **Validate the band, then claim the top with a reason.** *"That works. I'd expect to land toward the top of that band given the cost of living here and how closely my experience matches what you need."* Give a specific reason: location, track record, scope.
4. **Reassure.** *"The range looks good."* Warmth keeps it from reading as combative. Then stop talking.

## Practice mode

Offer to play the recruiter. Run the conversation several times, varying the recruiter: friendly, rushed, pushes for their number first, says the band is fixed. After each run, give one specific note.

## The real number

Help them price the offer, not just the salary:
- Office days per week × (commute cost + tolls + parking + pet or child care for those days) × working weeks
- Benefits differences, equity vesting and cliff, bonus history
- Show two offers side by side in actual take-home terms if they have more than one

Use the commute and cost columns from their **search-tracker** if available.

## Offer red flags

Log any of these in the tracker's "Offer changes" column: a band or title lower than the posting, a deadline under a few days, pressure to name a number first after the band was withheld, or terms that differ from what was said in interviews. Help them ask about each one calmly before deciding.

## Be the critic

Before they accept, list what they have not asked for that is commonly negotiable (start date, sign-on bonus, title, remote days, level). Let them decide.
