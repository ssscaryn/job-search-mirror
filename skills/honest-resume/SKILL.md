# Honest Resume

The model writes to sound impressive. The interview panel reads to find the claim that collapses. This skill keeps the confidence and strips the claims that will not survive a real question from someone who does the job. The question behind every line: **is this me, or a portrait I can defend?**

## Inputs to ask for

- Their LinkedIn profile (export or paste)
- One or more past resumes, including ones that did not work
- The job description
- Their **my-standards** claim ledger, if it exists

If any are missing, ask once for all of them before starting.

## Process

1. **Read my-standards.** Follow its writing rules and claim ledger exactly.
2. **Pick the lane.** Name the kind of role this posting belongs to, in the job seeker's own terms (for example product design versus UX research, clinical versus nurse management, data engineering versus analytics). Lead with experience from that lane, even if they have more in another. A reader rarely credits two careers at once.
3. **Draft the tailored version.** Keep their existing layout. If you have not seen it rendered, ask for a recent file and match it.
4. **Run the truth audit.** Produce a table, one row per claim:

   | Claim | Rating | Why | Question a panel would ask |
   |---|---|---|---|

   - **Green:** in the ledger or in their own past materials, specific, and defensible.
   - **Yellow:** true but stretched in scope, number, or ownership. Suggest the honest wording.
   - **Red:** not supported by anything they gave you (an invented tool, language, metric, or title). Remove it or ask.

5. **Ask before fixing reds.** One batched set of questions. Never resolve a red by guessing.
6. **Mark the reach.** If the role is a stretch, say so plainly and help them send it with conviction anyway. Reaching is allowed. Inventing is not.
7. **Log it.** Remind them to save the exact submitted version with the company name and date. The prep card for this job will need it.

## LinkedIn alignment

When asked to align LinkedIn: the headline and About section should point at the same lane as their best converting resumes (check the tracker if available). Interviewers probe gaps between the resume and the profile. If they are searching two lanes, write a headline that holds both without leaning on either.

## Be the critic

Before you call anything strong, list what a skeptical hiring manager would doubt. Only then say what works.
