# Interview Debrief

In the interview they were actively talking. Now they get to actively listen. Athletes watch their film after every game; this is the film.

## Consent first

Before analysing a transcript, confirm the recording was made with the interviewer's knowledge. California and several other US states require every party's consent, and it is good form everywhere: tell them a note taker is running and give them the chance to decline. If they did not get consent, work from their written notes instead.

## Attribution check (do this before any analysis)

AI note takers often credit a line to the wrong speaker, especially when people talk over each other. A misattributed line can make a weak interview read as a strong one.

1. List every passage where the speaker is uncertain or the content seems out of character.
2. Ask the user to confirm who said each one. The interview is fresh in their mind.
3. Only then continue.

## Question by question

For every question they were asked:

| # | Question (verbatim) | Their answer (short) | Landed? | Stronger answer | Story bank link |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **Landed?** Yes / partly / no, with the evidence: did the interviewer follow up, move on fast, or lean in?
- **Stronger answer:** in their voice, true to their claim ledger, under 90 seconds spoken.
- Mark answers that rambled and where they buried the lead.

## Game tape summary

- What made them lean in
- What fell flat (be specific; this section must never be empty)
- Proof points to follow up on in a thank-you note
- Which voice they used (specialist, adjacent field, business, recruiter friendly) and whether it fit the person

## Two lists for next time

1. **Questions asked of them**, verbatim. These predict the next round.
2. **Questions they asked**, with the answers they got. Pull out the interviewer's operating manual: how they weigh risk, what failure looks like to them, what they need in the first year, their exact vocabulary.

Hand both lists to **prep-card** and new or improved stories to **story-bank**.

## Red flags to log

Pull these from the transcript and write them into the tracker row for this company, so the job seeker never has to type them. State each one as an observation with the evidence, never as a verdict.

- **Interviewers:** how many were in the room.
- **Panel observation:** ask the job seeker how they would describe the panel as a whole (for example "all men"). **Never infer gender, race, or age from names or voices.** Record their words, for the panel, never for a named person.
- **Possibly unlawful questions:** any question about age, children or family plans, marital status, religion, national origin, disability or health, pregnancy, or salary history (illegal to ask in California and a growing number of states). Quote the question. Note that laws vary by location and this is a flag, not a legal finding.
- **Resume misstated:** anything an interviewer said the resume claims that it does not. Quote both the line and the resume.
- **Process signals:** number of rounds so far, total interview hours, any take-home assignment and its length, interviews the company rescheduled, and scope that changed between rounds.

If something in the interview was clearly mistreatment, offer to help draft candidate experience feedback using specifics from the transcript.

## Going deeper (optional)

Offer a word by word pass on any single answer: which phrase created the follow-up, where attention dropped, which sentence to cut.

## Be the critic

Lead with what fell flat. Argue the interviewer's side. Only after that, say what went well. If the interview went badly because the interviewer behaved badly, help them document specifics from the transcript; candidate experience feedback is worth sending.
